Gospel Conversation Guide

Introduction
This practical guide gives short, Bible-centred steps you can use in everyday conversations. Keep it simple, listen well, point to Scripture, and invite a response.

1) Start with a warm question and listen
- Example: "How have you been? What gives your life meaning?" Listen more than you speak. Jesus met people where they were (John 4:7-26).

2) Hear their story and reflect
- Ask one or two follow-ups to understand their heart. Reflect back: "It sounds like..." This builds trust and shows you care (James 1:19).

3) Share the core gospel clearly (use the biblical outline)
- Creation: God made us good and to be in relationship with him (Genesis 1:26-27). 
- Problem: Sin breaks our relationship with God (Romans 3:23).
- Consequence: Sin leads to death and separation (Romans 6:23).
- Rescue: Jesus lived, died, and rose again to pay for sin and restore us (Romans 5:8; 1 Corinthians 15:3-4).
- Response: Turn from sin and trust Jesus Christ as Lord and Savior (Romans 10:9-10; Acts 2:38).

Short script you can use:
"God loves you. We all fall short and need help (Romans 3:23). Jesus died and rose to rescue us (Romans 5:8; 1 Corinthians 15:3-4). You can turn to Jesus by repenting and trusting him (Romans 10:9). Would you like to pray with me?"

4) Invite a response and next steps
- Offer a clear next step: pray, read a short passage (John 3 or Romans 6), meet again, or attend church. Ask: "Would you like to pray right now?" or "Can we read John 3 together?" (Matthew 28:19-20).

5) A short prayer template
"Lord Jesus, I know I'm a sinner. I believe you died and rose for me. Please forgive me, come into my life, and help me follow you. Amen." (Use this only as a guide; let people speak in their own words.)

6) Follow up and disciple
- Connect them to a local church, a small group, or a short discipleship plan (2 Timothy 2:2). Follow up in a few days to pray and encourage.

Helpful Scripture references
- John 3:16; Romans 3:23; Romans 6:23; Romans 5:8; Romans 10:9-10; Matthew 28:18-20; Acts 2:38.

Practical tips
- Keep it short and clear. Avoid theological jargon. Focus on Jesus and the Bible. Pray for the Holy Spirit to guide the conversation (John 16:13).
